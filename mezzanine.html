The mezzanine measurement — for Tamas

Ten minutes · nothing is powered · you cannot break anything or hurt yourself · this unblocks the whole carrier board
What this is, in one line: the module-to-base connector has no orientation mark, so we prove where two known signals physically sit by making the meter beep, and photograph the answer.

Grab

1One mesh-kit base board from the DigiKey box (leave unit 3 alone — it's working)
2The RAK4631 module unclipped from it — we probe the EMPTY socket
3Multimeter + the needle tips
4Phone for photos · good light · reading glasses if they help — the feet are tiny

Step 1 — the reference photo (do this FIRST)

Bare board flat on the bench, USB connector pointing toward you (down in the photo). Take one sharp photo of the whole board. Every position we record is described against THIS photo — it's the map everything hangs off. Don't move the board after; if you do, same orientation again.

Step 2 — meter on beep mode

Turn the dial to the symbol that looks like a sound wave / speaker (often shares a spot with the diode arrow — press the mode button until the display shows the sound symbol). Touch the two probe tips together: it should beep. That's the whole skill. Nothing is powered at any point.

Step 3 — know your target

The mezzanine socket is the small connector in the middle of the board where the module clips on. Don't poke inside it — the pins bend. Instead work on its solder feet: the two neat rows of tiny metal tabs along its long edges, 20 per side. In your reference photo orientation, call them the LEFT row and RIGHT row, feet numbered 1–20 counting from the TOP (the end away from the USB).

Step 4 — first signal: battery +

4aOne needle tip pressed onto the battery connector's + pin (the JST socket — the + is marked on the silkscreen; it's also the pin the red wire goes to). Hold it there.
4bOther needle tip: touch each solder foot in turn. Start top-left, work down; then the right row. Slow, one foot at a time.
4cWhen it beeps — note the position ("LEFT row, feet 1 and 2" style). Battery + usually lands on two or three neighbouring feet — that's normal and expected, record all of them.
4dPhoto of the connector with your probe on a beeping foot (or mark the positions on a screenshot after).
The one trap: a fat or tilted tip touching TWO feet at once fakes a beep on the wrong pin. Check yourself: probe two feet next to a beeping one — if everything beeps, you're bridging; use the very point of the needle.

Step 5 — second signal: an IO pad at the other end

One signal can't tell a flip from a match — we need a second, from a different part of the connector. Find a silkscreen-labelled through-hole on the board — names like IO1, IO2, TXD, SW1, 3V3 printed next to small holes (on the slot/header areas). Pick ONE with a clear label — IO1 is ideal. Needle tip into (or onto) that hole, then hunt the feet again exactly like step 4. An IO signal should beep on exactly one foot. Record label + position + photo.
If you can't find a labelled IO hole on the mini base, use GND instead (battery connector's − pin): it beeps on SEVERAL feet spread around — photograph which ones. The pattern of many-GND + the battery-pair is still enough for Tamas to orient the connector.

Step 6 — the bonus photo Tamas can't get anywhere else

From the drawer: one loose AXK socket (the DigiKey bag marked AXK5F40547YG). Photograph it next to the underside of the RAK4631 module, close up, so the little notch/keying and the corner shapes of both connectors are visible in one frame. This is the physical footprint reference his datasheets don't show.

Step 7 — what to send

Item
1Reference photo (whole board, USB at bottom)
2Your notes, e.g.: BAT+ → LEFT row, feet 1+2 · IO1 → RIGHT row, foot 7 (counting from the top/away-from-USB end)
3Close-up photos of the beeping positions
4The loose-socket-vs-module photo
5One line: "measured on the RAK19007 base, module removed, board unpowered"
That's the whole job. If anything beeps in a way that seems weird — everything beeps, nothing beeps, the counts don't make sense — stop and describe it; weird results are information too (nothing you can do with a beeper can damage the board).
