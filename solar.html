Solar characterisation rig — detailed wiring

One panel · CN3791 · one 18650 · ESP32-S3-DevKitC (battery powered, deep-sleeps) · IP67 box · logs to Home Assistant
Golden rules: wire everything dead (no cell in the holder, panels covered or face-down) · panels connect last · every ground ends up on the same breadboard rail · nothing above 3.3 V ever touches a GPIO directly.

THE SIMPLE VERSION — build it in 10 steps

UPDATE — you have the screw-terminal shield (the green board): even easier. Forget the breadboard for connections — every ESP pin is a labelled screw terminal. The build becomes:
· Battery power: BAT+ wire into the terminal marked 5Vin, battery − into GND. Done — no rails.
· Divider A (panel): twist one leg of the 100k and one leg of the 22k together with a short wire and screw all three into terminal 6. The 100k's other leg goes to panel +, the 22k's other leg into a GND terminal.
· Divider B (battery): same trick into terminal 4 — 100k's far leg to 5Vin's wire (BAT+), 22k's far leg to GND.
· Charge sense: 10k from the CN3791 CHRG pad, its other leg into terminal 5.
All three signals sit on neighbouring terminals 4 · 5 · 6. Screw terminals grip resistor legs properly — this will survive the IP67 box far better than any breadboard. The diagrams below still show the breadboard version; the electrical connections are identical.
Big picture: your solar → charger → battery wiring from the unit rig does not change at all. You are only ADDING the logger: the ESP board, 5 resistors and 8 wires. If a step says a colour band, that's the stripes painted on the resistor.

The 10 steps, in order — battery OUT of the holder until step 10

#Do this
1Wire from the battery holder's red lead junction → breadboard RED rail
2Wire from the battery holder's black lead junction → breadboard BLUE rail
3Wire: RED rail → ESP pin printed 5V
4Wire: BLUE rail → ESP pin printed GND
5Divider A: 100k (yellow stripe) — one leg in the row where the panel + wire arrives, other leg into an empty row = meeting point A. Then 22k (red-red-orange) — one leg into meeting point A, other leg to the BLUE rail
6Divider B: the other 100k — one leg on the RED rail, other leg into a different empty row = meeting point B. The other 22k — one leg into meeting point B, other leg to the BLUE rail
7Wire: meeting point A → ESP GPIO6
8Wire: meeting point B → ESP GPIO4
9The 10k (brown-black-orange): one leg soldered/hooked to the CN3791's CHRG LED pad, the other leg to a wire → ESP GPIO5
10Look over steps 1–9 once more, then battery into the holder. ESP light flickers = alive. Photo → me → I flash it from here (or you run one command)
Only three ways to get this wrong: panel or battery + wired straight to a GPIO without going through its resistor pair (steps 5–6 exist for a reason) · the 10k missing from step 9 · USB plugged in while wire 3 is connected (pull wire 3 off the 5V pin whenever USB goes in).

Three ways to let the smoke out, avoided: 6 V panel voltage straight into a GPIO (always through Divider A) · USB and battery-rail-to-5V at the same time (pull the 5V wire when flashing) · CHRG pad without its 10k (node sits ~4 V when idle).
Deep-sleep gotcha, so it doesn't surprise you: the ESP is only awake 25 s in every 5 min. To reflash later, either catch it awake (watch HA for the entities updating) or flip the prevent deep sleep switch ESPHome creates in HA, then flash on the next wake.
Recording: three HA entities — panel V, battery V, Solar Charging on/off. History gives charging-hours/day and the battery curve; that's the panel verdict. Config: solar-test/solar-logger.yaml (INA219 block ready, commented out).
