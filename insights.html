What we are learning

  • A container nobody collects is a signal in itself. When its neighbours keep being taken and it stays put, it has almost certainly been inspected and rejected — a contamination measure per location, without inspection records.
  • The collector network builds itself. Collections became faster every week without any intervention. The service is becoming part of people's routine.
  • Placement matters. Demand varies considerably between positions only tens of metres apart. The data will show which positions earn their place.
  • Five-minute logging is the right resolution. Fine enough for the daily rhythm, full-capacity periods and the contamination signature; each unit holds months of history.

Next steps

Troubleshooting continues unit by unit. The second monthly report will carry measured fleet numbers in place of the projections, plus what the data says about placement and automatic contamination flagging.
